For clarity, the applicable Smarkets Terms and Conditions state the following:

Rule 2.14
"You must keep your login credentials secret at all times, and you remain solely responsible for the security and confidentiality of your account."

Rule 2.16
"If a bet is placed using your account, the bet will be considered to have been made by you and therefore once accepted by us, it shall be considered valid unless it contravenes any other term."

The bets that reduced your balance were placed while logged in with your login credentials and were accepted at the time. Under Rule 2.16 they are therefore treated as having been placed by you and stand as valid. This reflects the position previously provided by our support team, which I have reviewed and confirm is correct under the Terms.

We have a record of the notifications you described, including the attempted sign in from the Netherlands on 7 July. We can also see that a new payment method was subsequently added to your account. As you noted, that card has now been removed and blacklisted so it cannot be used again. However, the location of an attempted sign in does not change how Rules 2.14 and 2.16 apply, as those rules turn on whether the correct credentials were used.

You also asked why Smarkets does not pursue the individual who obtained the password and where the funds went. We do not have the ability to trace funds beyond the platform or to pursue a third party who may have obtained credentials externally, and we cannot reverse settled bets that were valid under the Terms.
